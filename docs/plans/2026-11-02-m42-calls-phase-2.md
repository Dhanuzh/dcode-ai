# M42 — Calls, phase 2 (v1.0.43)

Status: implemented

## 1. Less echo without headphones

When your speakers play someone's voice, your microphone hears it a moment
later and would send it back. Each person's call now remembers how loud
its speakers just played; a microphone frame that is quieter than that
(scaled) within ~250 ms counts as echo and isn't sent. You still get
through by speaking up over someone (double talk), the way Teams-style
"ducking" works. `/call echo off` turns it off (with headphones).

It isn't true echo cancellation (no C++ library): very loud speakers next to
the microphone can still leak, and talking over someone needs a louder
voice.

## 2. Push-to-talk and volume per person

- `/call ptt` switches push-to-talk on or off. While it's on, the
  microphone sends only while you hold **Ctrl+Space**. A held key repeats in
  every terminal; the mic stays open ~0.6 s after the last repeat, so
  releasing it closes the mic. The footer shows `hold ctrl+space` /
  `● talking`.
- `/call volume <name> <0-200>`: how loud someone is for you (100 =
  normal). Only on your side.
- `/call` alone, while in a call, lists who's in it and your settings.

## 3. Weak Wi-Fi

- **Adaptive jitter buffer**: each speaker starts at 60 ms. Every time
  their audio runs dry mid-sentence, it grows by 20 ms (up to 300 ms); after
  10 quiet seconds it shrinks back. Steady networks stay fast, and bad ones
  stop stuttering.
- **Concealment**: a gap is filled with the last 20 ms, faded, instead of a
  click of silence.
- **No backlog**: audio is dropped instead of queued when a connection
  falls behind (more than ~0.5 s waiting, on the host per guest and on the
  guest's side). Late audio is worse than lost audio. After a reconnect, old
  audio isn't replayed.

(A smaller codec, Opus, would need a C library; UDP would need a second
firewall rule. Neither is in this round.)

## 4. Local `dcode-ai join` terminals get the same as invited guests

- When the host's TUI starts, it also opens a private invite on
  127.0.0.1, an ephemeral port with a random key. The key is written to
  `<runtime dir>/<session>.join` (mode 0600 on Unix, so only your own user
  can read it).
- `dcode-ai join <id>` uses it when present: you get the full interface,
  calls, chat and to-dos. No knock is needed (only your own user can read
  the key), and you may approve and stop like the host.
- Without the file (an older host, or no TUI), `join` works as before.
- All calls share one call: the host, LAN guests and local joiners hear each
  other.

## Tests

- Mixer:
  - the buffer grows after an underrun and shrinks after quiet;
  - a gap is concealed, not cut to silence.
- Echo gate: a frame quieter than recent playback is dropped; a louder one
  passes.
- Push-to-talk: no frames while released; frames while held; closed again
  ~0.6 s after.
- Invite:
  - an auto-admit (local) host lets a guest in without knocking;
  - a slow guest's audio queue doesn't grow past its limit.
- PTY: the host and a local `join` in a full interface, both in `/call`,
  `/call ptt`, `/call volume`, `/hangup`.
